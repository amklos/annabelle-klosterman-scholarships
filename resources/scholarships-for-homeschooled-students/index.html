Can Homeschooled Students Get Scholarships?

Yes. Homeschooled students can apply for many scholarships. Learn what eligibility details to check and how to prepare application materials.

By Annabelle Klosterman · Scholarship guidance for students and families

Yes, homeschooled students can apply for scholarships. Some opportunities are open to students regardless of school type, while others have specific enrollment, school nomination, or transcript requirements. The important step is to read the eligibility rules and ask the sponsor how its requirements apply to your education path.

Do not rule yourself out just because you are homeschooled. Check the actual eligibility requirements for each scholarship.

1. Look for scholarships open to all students

Many scholarship programs focus on interests, community involvement, intended majors, career goals, leadership, creative work, or personal experiences. These may not depend on attending a traditional high school. Search for opportunities that match the student’s profile and confirm the rules with the sponsor.

2. Read school and enrollment requirements carefully

Some scholarships require applicants to attend a particular high school, receive a nomination from a school counselor, or be enrolled in an accredited program. Others ask for proof of graduation or a transcript. If the wording is unclear, contact the scholarship provider before investing time in the application.

3. Prepare clear academic records

Homeschooled students may use a parent-prepared transcript or another record of coursework, depending on the applicable requirements and the college or scholarship provider. Keep course titles, academic years, grades or evaluations, credits where appropriate, and any supporting documentation organized. Confirm the format each organization accepts.

4. Show learning beyond a traditional classroom

Applications may ask about activities, work, volunteering, projects, leadership, or community involvement. Homeschool students can describe meaningful experiences from classes, independent study, dual enrollment, jobs, family responsibilities, clubs, competitions, service, and personal projects. Explain what you did and what you learned.

5. Plan for recommendations

If a scholarship requires recommendations, think about adults who can speak specifically about the student’s work and character. Depending on the sponsor’s rules, this might include an instructor, coach, employer, volunteer supervisor, mentor, or community leader. Ask early and provide the recommender with the scholarship details and deadline.

6. Ask questions when a requirement seems unclear

Do not guess whether a homeschooled student qualifies. Send the scholarship organization a short, specific question. Explain the education arrangement and ask what documentation it accepts. Keep the response with the scholarship notes.

A quick checklist for homeschool applicants

  • Confirm whether the scholarship is open to homeschooled students.
  • Check transcript, graduation, enrollment, and nomination requirements.
  • Prepare an organized academic record and supporting materials.
  • List activities, projects, work, volunteering, and responsibilities.
  • Identify possible recommenders and ask well before the deadline.
  • Save answers from scholarship providers when requirements are unclear.

Homeschooling is one part of a student’s education story, not an automatic barrier to scholarship opportunities. Focus on the requirements, prepare clear records, and look for awards that fit the student’s interests and experiences.

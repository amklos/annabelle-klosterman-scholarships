Ways to work together

Choose the kind of support that works for your student.

Choose personalized one-on-one guidance, a focused class or workshop, or a semester-long program with structure and accountability.

01 · PERSONALIZED SUPPORT
One-on-one coaching

Start with the student, not a generic checklist.

We start with a conversation about the student’s experiences, interests, activities, strengths, and goals. From there, we identify themes worth highlighting, build a shortlist of relevant scholarships, and map out next steps. Coaching can include profile review, scholarship-fit research, application planning, and feedback on selected essays or application responses.

What we can cover
  • Review the student’s background and identify strengths to highlight
  • Find and prioritize scholarships that fit the student
  • Get feedback on select applications and essay responses

Students get tools and guidance to handle the work themselves. I’m there to help them make the most of it.

02 · ONE-TIME SESSION
Classes & workshops

Learn a strategy. Leave with a plan.

Choose a focused topic, like finding scholarships, building a stronger student profile, or writing application essays, or use a session to walk through the scholarship process from start to finish. Students work through practical examples, identify their next steps, and leave with tools they can put to use right away. Workshops can be adapted for a class, student group, or family audience.

What you’ll work on
  • Understand what makes a student stand out
  • Learn how to find scholarships that fit
  • Set up a simple system for applications
  • Leave with practical takeaways and a clear idea of what to do next

A one-time class or workshop is designed to teach, clarify, and help students know what to do next.

03 · SEMESTER PROGRAM
Semester scholarship strategy program

Make progress with structure and support.

This is for students who want more than a one-time conversation. Across a semester, the group works through the process in stages: understanding each student’s strengths, building a targeted scholarship list, organizing deadlines, and making progress on applications and essays. Students complete work between recurring sessions, bring questions and drafts for feedback, and benefit from shared momentum and accountability.

How we’ll use the time
  • Build a strong student profile and scholarship strategy
  • Create a targeted scholarship list and application plan
  • Work on applications and essays between sessions
  • Get feedback and keep making progress at recurring sessions

Students work through the process together, get feedback along the way, and leave with a way to keep going after the program ends.

How I approach scholarships

A practical process for making the most of your time and effort.

01
Build your candidate profile

Take stock of the student’s background, experiences, activities, interests, and goals. Those details help show what makes them different.

02
Apply strategically

Look for scholarships that match the student. A well-chosen application is usually a better use of time than sending out dozens that aren’t a fit.

03
Use a system

Keep deadlines, essays, and application materials in one place so you’re not hunting everything down each time you apply.

My story

I wasn’t the perfect student. I learned how to play the game.

I was homeschooled, my family wasn’t planning to fund college, and I had to figure out the process for myself. I learned how to position my story, search strategically, and stay organized.

Then · The search

I stopped treating scholarships like a numbers game.

I looked for opportunities that made sense for my background, interests, experiences, and goals instead of applying to every scholarship I came across.

What changed · A system

I built a system I could actually stick with.

A tracker and organized application materials helped me keep up with deadlines, reuse parts of my applications, and keep going when I heard nothing back or got a rejection.

Now · The work I do

Now I help students find an approach that works for them.

I work in cybersecurity, but this isn’t just for STEM students. I help students see what they have to offer, find opportunities that fit, and build skills they can use in college, work, and beyond.

Questions are welcome

Let’s be real about scholarships.

It’s okay to ask how this works before you decide if it’s right for your family.

My promiseUseful advice, honest expectations, and the student’s own voice.
Do I need good grades?

No. I definitely wasn’t a 4.0 student, and plenty of scholarships aren’t based on merit or grades. We can look for opportunities that fit a student’s interests, experiences, and goals.

Is this only for STEM students?

No. I have experience with STEM and cybersecurity scholarships, but students in every field can use these ideas. The best opportunities depend on the individual student, not just their intended major.

Will you do the applications for my student?

No. The goal is to teach students how to approach the process themselves. I can help review select applications and essays, offer feedback, and show them how to strengthen their work while keeping their own voice.

Can I join as a parent?

Absolutely. Parents are welcome to be part of the conversation and the plan.

Can you guarantee that my student will win scholarships?

No. Scholarship decisions are made by the organizations offering the awards. I can help students understand their strengths, choose scholarships carefully, and put their best work forward. I can’t promise that they’ll receive an award.

The story behind my approach

I know the scholarship search from the student side.

I was homeschooled, so I had to figure out the process without a traditional school counselor. I wasn’t valedictorian and I didn’t have perfect test scores. I started researching, keeping track of deadlines, and learning how to make my applications stronger.

I applied to more than 50 scholarships and earned over $525,000. I was offered three full rides. My scholarships were worth more than my tuition. After the university paid my bill, it sent me the extra funds in checks. With the stipends too, I was paid to go to college. I later built a career in cybersecurity, where I saw how much STEM scholarships can open doors.

Now I help students and families narrow down their options, focus on scholarships that make sense, and make a plan they can follow. I won’t promise a particular award or write a student’s essay for them. I will give honest feedback and help them show their own strengths.
